Did the tune look right, and did any alarm go off?

Two logs: the dash's from 26 Oct 2026 (Monticello) and the ECU's own from 27 Apr 2025. Both are run data and stay local; this page records what was read out of them. Where a check could not be made, it says so and why.

The dash log is the session alone: 42–1,358 s, an out-lap and three laps. The file also carries 1,614 copies of its last three seconds, which the reader now drops; see 01-dash-log-2026-10-26.md. Figures first written on this page were counted with those copies, and every one below has been recomputed without them.

The tune

Ignition did what the 1.153 tune says. Above 2,000 rpm the logged ignition angle matches the IgnAdv Table 1 of the tune embedded in the April 2025 ECU log to 0.06° median, within 0.15° on 82% of samples. Against the two committed tunes it is 0.31° and 46%, so the car is not running either of them.

The timing trims are the acceleration correction — probable. The ECU pulled up to 5° on 18% of samples above 2,000 rpm, under load: median 4,518 rpm at 76% throttle. The 1.153 tune's Ignition acc correction is enabled: when the throttle opens faster than 25%/s it retards 2° (at 100%/s) to 5° (at 500%/s and up), holds for 4 ignition events and decays 1° per event. 91% of the pulls come with a throttle step that fast, in the half second before or into the next sample, and most are 1–2°, the decay. The rest, 118 samples, are under 1.2° (median 0.6°). A first pass put this at 77%: it smoothed the throttle rate over two samples, which hid the small, quick blips behind the shallow pulls. It is not knock (KnockCtrl Enable and KnockCtrl Retard Enable are both 0), not temperature (MTune's Ignition correction tables give 0° across the intake 26.7–34.8 °C and coolant 73.6–97.2 °C seen), not idle control (only below 4% throttle and 2,400 rpm), and not a shift cut (disabled).

Nothing reached a limit. Peak 7,368 rpm against a rev limit of 7,500 (control range 200, from the 1.153 tune). Fuel cut and ignition cut are 0 for the whole session. Coolant ran 73.6–97.2 °C and intake air 26.7–34.8 °C; the ECU's error channel is 0 throughout.

The mixture was rich, and the ECU thought it was lean — probable. The ECU's Lambda A read 1.8–2.4 above 2,000 rpm and never below 1.68, and closed loop added +5% fuel on 30% of those samples, 39% at full throttle. The dash's own LambdaCAN read about 0.74 over the same range: 0.73 at idle, 0.74 from 3,000 rpm up, at its 0.65 minimum on 15% of samples above 5,000 rpm. The ECU's target in April 2025 was about 0.87. Details in 01-dash-log-2026-10-26.md. The logged lambda target is missing from this file because its CAN frame (0x527) never arrived.

The fuel map itself runs rich — probable. In the April 2025 ECU log, when the ECU's own sensor was still reading, Lambda A sat at 0.69 (median) against a target of 0.87 above 2,000 rpm, richer than target on 95% of rows. Closed loop could not close the gap: it is only allowed in above 90 °C coolant, was inactive on most rows, and when active sat at −5%, its limit. So the base fuelling is about 20% richer than its own target, and the dash's 0.74 in October 2026 agrees. That is a question for whoever tunes the car, and it is the context for the injector duty below.

What the ECU is told over CAN is placeholders. The ECU takes two inputs from the dash on frame 0x1: "Fuel Pressure", which feeds fuel pressure sensor 1, and "Left Lambda", routed to lambda bank B (cylinders 1–3). The dash's CAN Output1 fills both slots with fixed values, not channels: 8 kPa and 0. So:

  • The ECU's fuel pressure is a constant 8 kPa — it read exactly 8.0 all through the April 2025 log. MTune's CAN input value 1 reads those two bytes as unsigned 16-bit at a resolution of 1, straight into "Fuel pressure sensor 1 (kPa)", so the dash's 8 arrives as 8 kPa with nothing in between. MTune's Fuel general screen has injector flow tracking that sensor ("Fuel pressure sensor 1 tracking", 286 cc/min at 3 bar, target 300 kPa). The real fuel pressure, which the dash measures on a channel expander, ran 3.24–3.98 bar (median 3.47) on 26 Oct. How the MaxxECU treats an implausible 8 kPa is not established; a straight square-root correction would make every pulse about six times longer, which plainly did not happen.
  • Bank B has no lambda. The tune runs "2 sensors/Dual VE", sensor A on the internal wideband's channel 1 for cylinders 4–6 and sensor B on channel 2 for cylinders 1–3. Lambda B read 0 throughout both logs, which is what the CAN input routed to bank B carries. So whatever closed loop does, it can only ever act on bank A.
  • The dash's own fuel pressure and LambdaCAN lambda are real, and neither is sent. Pointing those two CAN slots at those channels, or turning the ECU's use of them off, is a change for whoever looks after the car.

The internal wideband is set to sensor type LSU 4.2, and MTune warns that a wrong setting can damage the sensor. Which Bosch sensor is fitted is not in any file; if it is an LSU 4.9, the setting is wrong and Lambda A's lean reading would be one result.

Closed loop can only move fuel 5% either way. In the 2024 tune it is off; in the 1.153 tune the car runs it is "Wideband (target=lambda table)". It acts only with coolant at 90 °C or more and 30 s after start, is switched off while the throttle is moving, and its correction range is −5% to +5% across the whole table, every rpm and throttle. The 26 Oct log agrees: the correction is never non-zero below 90 °C coolant, and sits at +5% on 30% of samples above 2,000 rpm. Fuel adaptation is disabled, so nothing else learns a correction.

Injector duty is worth a look — probable. It peaked at 88.1%, above 80% on 91 samples and above 85% on 20, all between 6,600 and 7,370 rpm. That is close to where an injector runs out of headroom. Some of it is the +5% the bad lambda reading was adding, so it may fall once the sensor is fixed. This reads the dash's FuelDut channel as a plain percentage, which has not been checked against MTune.

Every alarm depends on the dash's own RPM input — not established. The "RPM" in these conditions is channel id 15, the MXP's RPM input on pin 15 of the 37-pin connector, not the ECU's rpm, which arrives over CAN. That channel is not in the 26 Oct log. The rev counter on display pages 2 and 3 and the shift lights use the same input. If the rev counter works, the input is live and the alarms arm as the table above says. If it does not, every alarm gated on "RPM > 500" can never fire, and Oil_Filter_Cold, gated on RPM < 500, lights whenever the filter is under 140 °F. See 02-whats-needed.md.

Sensors that do not look right

These are readings, not faults found on the car:

  • Oil filter temperature, 20.8–29.2 °C all session, while the oil tank ran 66.6–88.9 °C. A filter cooler than the tank by 40 °C on track reads as a sensor that is not measuring the filter. Every oil-filter alarm depends on it.
  • RR_Inside is flat at −13.2 for the whole session — a channel with nothing behind it, or a dead sensor.
  • Track_Temp reads −24.5 to −3.3 °C (median −9.4) on a day the intake air was 27–35 °C.

What the EPM08's outputs did

The power module logs each output's state, its current and a health status. All six read "ok" for the whole session: no output tripped or faulted.

Both switching outputs did what their triggers say. The trans pump's is rpm above 1,500 (with a 2 s off-delay) or GPS speed above 6.4 km/h; it read ON at a median 4,400 rpm and OFF at idle. The trans fan's includes the transmission above 60 °C; it switched on with the transmission at 60.1 °C. The statuses are recorded about every 2 seconds, so around a switch the state and the current can disagree for a moment.

What switches the EPM08's outputs

The keypad does most of it. The coils, LCU and injector outputs are triggered by IGN == S1: button 1 on the K8 keypad (serial 1900122), named "IGN". The fuel pump is triggered by the EPM08's own FP_Trigger input. Wipers 1 and 2 are set never-on, which is why neither is in the 26 Oct log. The page showed all three of the keypad's outputs as FP Trigger == S1 until 27 Oct 2026: the EPM08's input and the K8's button share a channel id and input number, and the reader ignored the module serial that tells them apart. That is fixed in parsers/aim_zconf.py.

The ECU's own errors (27 Apr 2025 log)

  • Error code count 0 and ignition cut never, across all 21,566 rows.
  • Three trigger sync losses, all with the engine stopped. The lost-sync count steps 0 → 3 between 693.6 s and 697.4 s, with rpm at 0 on every row that is out of sync. That is the engine stopping or restarting, not a fault while running.
  • 23 fuel cuts, all on overrun. 3,532–4,850 rpm with the throttle closed on 98% of the cut rows; the two exceptions are the row the throttle reopens on.

The 26 Oct dash log carries the same ECU channels over CAN: error 0, fuel cut 0, ignition cut 0 throughout.
