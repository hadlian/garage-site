The 26 Oct 2026 dash log — what it settles, and what it shows

One AiM .xrk from the MXP, recorded 26 Oct 2026 at Monticello (track "MMC Full", session "Generic testing"), 181 channels. It is run data and stays local like every log here; this page records what was read out of it. It was read with parsers/aim_xrk.py, using the file's own group table to name the ECU stream, which is now what the reader does by default.

What it settles

The committed dash configuration is the one in the car. Every expansion module the .zconf2 declares with a serial number appears in the log under the same serial, with the same channel names:

ModuleSerialIn the log
LambdaCAN1011938yes
Channel Expander (×6)2001138, 2002023, 2000317, 2001145, 2001272, 2002012yes, all six
K8 keypad1900122yes
EPM083800108yes, with its outputs named: fuel pump, coils, trans pump, trans fan, LCU, injectors

The shift light and the GS dash carry serial 0 in the configuration and log no channels of their own. Placeholder channels the configuration leaves unnamed (0Ch02, 2Ch04 and the like) are not logged, which is consistent with nothing being wired to them. That last point is a reading, not a check.

The ECU and the dash agree on the CAN link. The tune sets CAN Protocol MaxxECU = 1, and the dash reads the ECU with AiM's "MAXXECU - CAN" driver on frames from 0x520 up, with 33 of the driver's 90 channels enabled. The ECU values in the log are those 33.

What it shows

The file is 134 MB, and the session is the first 28.5 MB of it. From byte 28,445,942 the file is one 64 KiB block — the last three seconds of the session — followed by 1,614 identical copies of it, then the closing metadata. parsers/aim_xrk.py now drops the copies (_load; see REPEATED_TAIL in formats/aim_xrk_map.json). Every figure on this page is from the session alone: 42–1,358 s, an out-lap of 787.9 s and laps of 176.4, 178.3 and 175.0 s. Counted with the copies, several figures first written here were badly wrong, and they have been corrected.

Neither committed tune is what the car runs — probable, and stronger than it was. The ECU's own firmware channel reads 11.53 throughout, which reads as firmware 1.153. The committed tunes are 1.149 (source/2024-5-13.MaxxECU-save) and 1.147 (the 2023 "after dyno" file). The ignition the car ran agrees: above 2,000 rpm the logged ignition angle matches the IgnAdv table of the 1.153 tune embedded in the 27 Apr 2025 MaxxECU log to 0.06° median, within 0.15° on 82% of samples. Against either committed tune it is 0.31° median and 46%. That tune already differs from the 2024 file in 27 settings, including lambda control (Lambdareg Type 0 → 2, Lambdareg MinCLT 10 → 90). What settles it is a tune saved from the ECU now. See 02-whats-needed.md.

Lambda A was not reading in this session — probable. Above 2,000 rpm, LambA reads exactly 2.4 on 32% of 7,278 samples and 1.80–2.13 on the rest (median 1.94); it never drops below 1.68. Over the same samples the lambda correction LaCorA sits at +5% on 30%: closed loop adding fuel to chase a reading of very lean. LambB is 0 throughout, because the CAN input feeding bank B is a fixed 0 from the dash (see 03-tune-and-alarms.md). In the 27 Apr 2025 MaxxECU log the same sensor read a plausible 0.63–0.90, with correction between −5% and 0.

The dash's own lambda sensor says the opposite: rich — probable. The LambdaCAN reads 1.0 for the first 20 seconds while it warms, then about 0.74: 0.73 at idle, 0.74 between 3,000 and 5,000 rpm and above 5,000. It is at exactly 0.65, its minimum, on 6% of samples (15% above 5,000 rpm). Its AFR channel is its lambda × 14.57 throughout, so the two agree with each other. The ECU's own lambda target, logged in April 2025 with the 1.153 tune, was about 0.87 above 2,000 rpm. So one sensor reads lean, one reads richer than target, and the ECU is acting on the lean one. Whether the two sensors sit in the same exhaust is not established.

Other ECU channels look ordinary: coolant 73.6–97.2 °C, intake air 26.7–34.8 °C, rpm to 7,368, error count 0 throughout. The expansion modules and the ECU's alarms are covered in 03-tune-and-alarms.md.

What the reader learned from this file

These were gaps in parsers/aim_xrk.py, now closed, and one that is not:

  • Expansion-module data is now read. It is in a record type no other log here uses ()(c, COMPACT_RECORD in the map). Decoded 27 Oct 2026 and confirmed three ways, none with a fitted number: the EPM08's total current equals the sum of its six outputs on 99.6% of samples, the LambdaCAN's AFR is its lambda × 14.57, and oil pressure tracks the ECU's rpm (correlation 0.97).
  • The repeated tail is dropped, which also fixed the lap list (1,619 laps became 4) and the stream timestamps.
  • Four ECU stream groups are empty because their CAN frames never arrived: 0x527 (accelerometers, lambda target), 0x528 (knock), 0x537 (fuel pressure) and 0x540 (virtual fuel tank). Every channel on those frames is missing and every channel on the other eight is present. Knock control is off in the 1.153 tune (KnockCtrl Enable = 0), which explains 0x528; the others are not explained.
  • The ECU stream is named from the file itself. stream() now names each slot from the log's own group table. The reader's built-in names, the E46 Canada's, apply only to a log whose table carries exactly that car's channels. Until 27 Oct 2026 they were applied to every log.

Also settled with the MaxxECU log

The MaxxECU tune's table row order, previously probable, is now confirmed: the 27 Apr 2025 log carries the tune it was running, and reading that tune's Lambda Table 1 the way the parser does reproduces the logged lambda target exactly on 17,276 of 21,302 samples, against 721 with the rows reversed. The evidence lives in formats/maxxecu_map.json under table_row_order, and parsers/test_maxxecu.py repeats the check.
