What the torque-management template establishes

The car's ECU is represented here by one file: a VCM Editor template (source/c7-corvette-torque-management.htt) exported from the car's tune, with VCM Editor's names export beside it. The tune itself is an encrypted .hpt the garage cannot read and does not hold. Everything below is read from the template's bytes by parsers/hp_tuners_htt.py; what a value means is stated only where the evidence says so.

VCM Editor's units are mixed: °C for 2861, N m for 2869, lb-ft for 12872, km/h for 12869's axis, and °F on other screens. Units may be set per category or carried by each parameter's definition.

  • What VCM Editor displays (literal): 491 °C for 2861, 6,041 N m for 2869, 96,671 lb-ft for 12872.

What the five traction bindings say, and what they don't

Read with their descriptions, the calibration says:

  • 2861: torque reduction for traction control is allowed only above a coolant temperature stored as 255 °C (491 °F; VCM Editor displays 491 °C). Either way it is unreachable as a normal coolant threshold.
  • 2869: the delivered-torque signal sent to traction control is scaled to a range stored as 8191 N m (6,041 lb-ft; VCM Editor displays 6,041 N m), far beyond any realistic delivered engine torque.
  • 289: traction control events may disable brake torque management.
  • 12872 and 12869: the brake torque limit is 96,671 lb-ft as displayed (stored 131068, labelled N m in the template) in every cell, with a multiplier of 1 everywhere. Brake torque management does not restrict engine torque through these two tables.

That is the ECM's calibration, not the whole traction system. The EBCM can still brake individual wheels, and torque may be reduced by paths these five parameters don't cover. Which values are factory is in 03-stock-comparison.md; how the car behaves is not established until it is logged. See 02-whats-needed.md.
